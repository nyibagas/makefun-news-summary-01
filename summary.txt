World news, Wednesday 11 November 2026.

Russia and Ukraine ended two days of US-mediated talks in Abu Dhabi by agreeing a large prisoner exchange, but with no breakthrough on territory; US envoys were to fly to Moscow to meet Vladimir Putin, and hours later Russian ballistic missiles struck Kyiv, damaging power facilities and a bridge (Reuters, Al Jazeera, Kyiv Independent).

At a Veterans Day service President Trump defended the war with Iran -- "America did not start this" -- as Iran's parliament speaker Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf said the Strait of Hormuz would "never return" to its pre-war status and Mohsen Rezaei set seven conditions for reopening it; Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent moved to a military base amid Iranian threats (AP, Al Jazeera, New York Times).

At COP31 in Antalya, UN chief Antonio Guterres warned "the climate crisis is outrunning us", island states demanded action, and an analysis found climate-finance pledges had fallen 70% since the last summit (Al Jazeera, Carbon Brief).

Spain held its first general strike in 14 years, with millions protesting housing costs and clashes reported (Guardian, Reuters).

Indonesia's vice-president, Gibran Rakabuming Raka, resigned as 120 lawmakers moved to impeach him over questioned education credentials (Reuters).

House Republicans blocked an effort to impeach Pentagon chief Pete Hegseth (Guardian).

Nvidia's board met to decide CEO Jensen Huang's future amid a leadership crisis at the chipmaker (Reuters).

In Congo, Ebola cases passed 11,000 in the deadliest outbreak on record (Reuters, NPR).

The SS United States, once the world's fastest ocean liner, was sunk off Florida to become the largest artificial reef (AP).

A US report showed Washington had received $12 billion from Venezuela's oil exports (New York Times).

Saudi Arabia and Turkey signed a deal for 3,000 MW of wind power (Reuters).
